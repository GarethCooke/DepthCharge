# Bench 2026-09-13 — CLK on a 10× probe: the owed measurement, two corrections, one defect

**What this is.** `hardware/BRINGUP.md:204`'s standing debt — *"Still owed: CLK on a 10× probe.
The 2026-08-11 finding is behavioural, not measured"* — paid. The M2 signal-integrity survey
used a **1× probe** and discounted its own edge readings in the same breath ("edge softening
partly attributable to the 1× probe", :105). This characterises GPIO 11 at the DevKit pin with a
compensated 10× probe at full bandwidth, and it changes three things that were written down wrong.

1. **CLK is 6.6667 MHz, not the "~2MHz" at `BRINGUP.md:101`** — and 6.6667 MHz is exactly
   `panel_actual_clock_hz()` (160 MHz ÷ 24). The firmware's own arithmetic is now
   instrument-confirmed to four significant figures.
2. **The edge times are the scope's, not the panel's.** Measured rise 4.11 ns mean against an
   instrument floor of 3.50 ns, with individual readings *below* that floor. Deconvolved, the
   real edge is **≈2.2 ns**, and the fastest ones cannot be measured on this scope at all.
3. **Undershoot reaches −760 mV** on the worst falling edge. That is below the input floor these
   shift registers specify, and it is the same clamp-diode path D7 caught phantom-powering the
   panel. **This is the finding that matters for M6.**

## 3 · The clock is 6.6667 MHz, and it is exactly what the firmware computes

`BRINGUP.md:101` records CLK as **"~2MHz"**. It is **6.66666 MHz** — 3.3× wrong, and wrong in
the direction that matters, because every argument in that file about edge rate and crosstalk
was made against the low number.

The measured figure is not a surprise once it is read against `firmware/src/panel_budget.hpp`:

    kS3LcdSourceHz / kS3LcdDivNum = 160 MHz / 24 = 6.6667 MHz

`platformio.ini:192` sets `-D S3_LCD_DIV_NUM=24`, and `panel_actual_clock_hz()` returns exactly
that. **The scope agrees to four significant figures, with σ 666 Hz over 11,689 measurements.**

That is worth more than the correction itself. `panel_budget.hpp:62-79` argues at length that the
library buckets `i2sspeed` and then reports a refresh rate computed from the nominal figure —
"every `refresh=105 Hz` this firmware has printed is therefore nominal and about 25 % low; the
real figure has been ~131 Hz". That analysis rested entirely on reading the library's source.
**It is now measured.** The divider override reaches the pin, and the derived refresh figures
that hang off `panel_actual_clock_hz()` can be trusted.

## 4 · The edges are instrument-limited, and the divider does not slow them

A 100 MHz scope has a rise-time floor of **0.35 ÷ 100 MHz = 3.50 ns**, and what it displays is
the root-sum-square of the real edge and its own. Frame A's **minimum rise of 3.22 ns is below
that floor** — arithmetically impossible for a real 3.22 ns edge, and therefore direct proof that
the instrument, not the signal, is setting these numbers.

Deconvolving `t_signal = √(t_measured² − 3.50²)`:

| | measured | deconvolved |
| --- | --- | --- |
| rise, typical | 4.11 ns | **≈2.15 ns** |
| fall, typical | 4.24 ns | **≈2.39 ns** |
| rise, fastest | 3.22 ns | *below the floor — unmeasurable here* |
| rise, slowest | 6.48 ns | ≈5.45 ns |
| fall, slowest | 6.70 ns | ≈5.71 ns |

So the honest statement is: **CLK transitions in about 2 ns, and this scope cannot resolve the
fast tail of the distribution.** Anything finer wants a 500 MHz instrument.

**This falsifies the stated mechanism for `S3_LCD_DIV_NUM=24`.** `platformio.ini:176` justifies
the divider as *"24 gives 160/24 = 6.67 MHz — a THIRD off the edge rate, which is the quantity
both open panel problems turn on"*. A clock divider changes the **repetition** rate, not the
**transition** time: rise time is set by the pad's drive strength into its load, and 2.15 ns is
what that pad does regardless of how often it is asked to do it. Two of the three reasons in that
comment survive intact — setup/hold margin genuinely improves (150 ns period against 100 ns), and
fewer transitions per second is genuinely less broadband noise for the radio — but "slower edges"
is not a thing that happened.

**Falsifier, cheap:** rebuild with `S3_LCD_DIV_NUM=16` and re-measure. If rise/fall come back
unchanged within σ (≈650 ps), the divider does nothing to edge rate and the comment should be
rewritten to the two reasons that hold. If they change, this paragraph is wrong and the comment
was right.

## 5 · The overshoot is large and the undershoot is out of spec

Against a 3V3 rail measured at **3.18–3.19 V** in `bench-2026-09-13-D7-switch-on.md`:

- **Overshoot:** peaks to **3.48 V typical**, **3.72 V worst** — roughly **+290 mV typical,
  +530 mV worst** above the rail that drives it.
- **Undershoot:** troughs to **−217 mV typical**, **−760 mV worst** — below the ground the panel
  and the DevKit share.

The undershoot is the defect. HUB75 receivers of this class specify an input floor around
**−0.3 V**, below which the input protection diode conducts. At −760 mV that diode is hard on, on
the worst falling edges, every 150 ns. **This is the same clamp-diode path D7 found conducting
in the other direction** — there, an unpowered panel was being phantom-powered through the
clamps from the driven lines; here, the same junctions are being forward-biased by the ringing.
One structure, two symptoms, and the second one is happening in normal operation with everything
powered correctly.

(The exact −0.3 V figure is the generic shift-register number. The FM6124 datasheet is not in
this repo; confirm it before quoting it in a design document.)

**The ringing is the interconnect's, not the probe's.** Post-edge ripple in both 5 ns/div frames
has a period of roughly **17–18 ns (≈55–60 MHz)**, read off the graticule rather than cursored.
A 15 cm ground clip (~150 nH) resonating against the probe's ~15 pF tip would ring at about
**106 MHz** — nearly twice as fast. The observed resonance is too slow to be the measurement
loop and sits in the range a 20 cm unterminated jumper pair into the panel's input capacitance
would produce. It is also comfortably inside the scope's 100 MHz bandwidth, so the amplitudes in
§2 are not being attenuated away.

## 6 · What this does not answer

- **It is measured at the source.** The probe was on the DevKit's GPIO 11 pin. What arrives at
  the panel's connector after 20 cm of unterminated jumper is a *different* waveform, and the
  reflection visible here is the evidence that it is different. The receiving end is unmeasured.
- **Only CLK.** R1, LAT and OE are still 1×-probe-only from M2 and still uncharacterised.
- **One configuration.** `clkphase = false`, divider 24, ladder running, one panel, one ribbon.
  No A/B against divider 16, none against `clkphase = true`, none against panel content.
- **Ground clip, not spring tip.** The ~57 MHz ring is too slow to be probe artefact, but the
  fine structure on the flat tops is not separable from it. A spring-tip ground would settle that.
- **Nothing about the right-edge artefact.** `BRINGUP.md:197` leaves that open and this
  measurement does not close it — it only supplies the edge rate the argument was missing.

## 7 · What it changes

- `BRINGUP.md:101` — "~2MHz" corrected to 6.67 MHz, with the 10× survey replacing the 1× one.
- `BRINGUP.md:204` — the debt is discharged; this file is the measurement.
- `platformio.ini:176` — "a THIRD off the edge rate" is wrong in its mechanism. Left in place
  pending the divider-16 falsifier in §4 rather than edited on this record's word alone.
- **M6 gains a requirement with evidence behind it: series termination, not just a ground plane.**
  The roadmap's `2× 74HCT245` buffers are load-bearing, but on their own they make this *worse* —
  an HCT output drives harder and transitions faster than the S3 pad now doing it, into the same
  unterminated line. **Put a series resistor (~33 Ω, value to be set against the trace impedance
  at layout) at each 245 output, on the board, adjacent to the buffer.** Without it the carrier
  buys a cleaner ground and a worse edge.
- **M6's panel-side load switch gets a second reason.** D7 argued it from phantom-powering; §5
  argues it from the same clamps conducting during normal operation. Series resistors attack both.
